Service charges in Dubai commercial towers: the line investors forget.

8 Oct 2026 · By the OwnStakeX team

Every commercial property investor in Dubai learns to ask about the rent. Fewer learn to ask about the service charge — the annual bill for running the building, which quietly decides what share of that rent you actually keep.

A service charge is the per-square-foot fee a building's management collects from owners each year to cover the common costs of the tower: security, cleaning, lifts, air-conditioning of common areas, landscaping, building insurance, and the management fee itself. In a commercial tower these costs are often higher than in a residential block, because lobbies run longer hours, air-conditioning loads are heavier, and the fit-out of shared facilities is more demanding.

The important thing to understand is where this charge sits in your yield calculation. Headline rental yields in Dubai are sometimes quoted on the gross rent — the full annual amount a tenant pays. But the owner pays the service charge out of that, along with insurance and, if the property is tenanted through an agent, a management fee. The yield that matters is the net yield: what remains after the costs that come with ownership. Two towers offering the same headline rent can produce noticeably different net yields if one charges substantially more per square foot to keep the lights on.

Why the charge varies so much

Service charges are set by the building's owners' association, based on an annual budget approved at a general meeting, and regulated through the RERA service-charge process. In practice they vary widely because they reflect the building's age, its facilities, and how it is managed. An older tower with ageing chillers spends more on maintenance; a trophy tower with a grand lobby and round-the-clock concierge costs more to run than a straightforward block.

This is why experienced investors read the service-charge history, not just this year's number. A building whose charges have risen steadily for five years is telling you something about its future. And the trend can run in either direction: a recently retrofitted tower may see charges stabilise, while one facing facade works or chiller replacement may be about to get more expensive.

The three questions to ask

  1. What is the charge per square foot, and what does it include? Ask for the budget breakdown. Chilled-water (cooling) charges in particular can be billed separately in some Dubai towers and catch first-time investors by surprise.
  2. Is there a sinking fund, and how healthy is it? Well-run towers collect extra each year into a reserve for major future repairs. A tower with no reserve is one special assessment away from an unexpected bill.
  3. How have charges moved over the last five years? A smooth, modest trend suggests disciplined management. Sharp jumps deserve an explanation — and a look at whether another is coming.

How it affects shared ownership

In a shared-ownership structure, the service charge is one line in the project's operating budget — but one of the largest, and most predictable. When you review any project's documents, find the opex section and check the service-charge assumption: is it this year's figure, or a realistic forward number? A budget that prices charges at today's rate and ignores five years of increases is flattering its own projections.

The service charge is never the reason to invest in an asset. But it is often the reason a deal looks better on paper than it performs in practice. Ask the three questions above before you commit, and the number in your yield calculation will be the honest one.
