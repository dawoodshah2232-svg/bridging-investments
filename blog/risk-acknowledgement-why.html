Why We Ask You to Sign a Risk Acknowledgement.

9 Oct 2026 · By the OwnStakeX team

Somewhere between choosing a project and confirming your reservation, we ask you to read and sign a risk acknowledgement. It is not a formality, and it is not there to scare you. Here is what it is, why we insist on it, and what happens if you decline.

What the document actually says

The risk acknowledgement is a short, plain-language statement of the realities of private-asset investing: capital is at risk, returns are not guaranteed, income can be interrupted, and your money may be committed for the full holding period. It lists the main risk factors of the asset class you are entering — concentration, illiquidity, operator dependence, and market cycles — in ordinary words, not legal code.

Why we ask before we accept your money

The timing is deliberate. We ask before your reservation becomes binding, because that is the last moment when the decision is fully yours and fully reversible. Reading the risks after you have committed is how regrets are made. Reading them before is how informed decisions are made.

There is a second reason: the acknowledgement creates a shared record. Months later, when a distribution is smaller than hoped or an asset hits a rough patch, nobody has to argue about what was understood. You said, in writing, that you understood the risks. We said, in writing, what they were. That record protects you as much as it protects the project — it keeps every conversation honest.

What it does not do

The acknowledgement does not waive your rights. It does not mean you agree to absorb losses caused by mismanagement or misrepresentation. It is a statement of informed consent, not a release of liability. If anything in the project is materially misrepresented to you, the acknowledgement does not protect the people who misled you.

If you don't want to sign

Then do not sign — and do not invest. That sounds blunt, but it is the point of the exercise. The acknowledgement is a filter. If the risks described feel unacceptable, that is valuable information, and walking away is the right call. We would rather lose a reservation today than welcome an investor who is surprised by a risk we disclosed.
