Five clauses to read in any subscription agreement.

8 Oct 2026 · By the OwnStakeX team

The subscription agreement is the contract you sign when you commit capital to a project. It is longer than the marketing pages, drier than the brochure — and far more important than both. Here are five clauses worth reading properly.

1. What you are actually buying

Every agreement should state precisely what your money buys: the number of shares or units, the class of those shares, and the rights attached to them. Do they carry voting rights? Are there different classes of investor with different terms? Read this clause the way you would read the description of a property: vague language here means you do not know what you own. The share class, the nominal value, and the rights attached should all be explicit.

2. The use of funds

The agreement should say where your money goes and when. Is your capital held until a minimum funding threshold is reached, or deployed immediately? What happens if the campaign does not fill — is there a long-stop date after which funds are returned? This clause is your protection against the limbo of money sitting neither invested nor refunded. A clear use-of-funds clause, with timelines and a return mechanism, is one of the strongest signs of a well-structured offer.

3. The fee clause

Every fee the platform or sponsor charges should be named here — management fees, performance or carried-interest terms, entry or exit fees, and any ongoing administration costs. Read it alongside the fee schedule, but treat the agreement as the binding version: if a fee appears in marketing but not in the contract, it is the contract that governs. Watch for vague phrases like "reasonable costs" or "other expenses as incurred" — these are the places where fees hide.

4. Distributions: how and when you get paid

This clause describes the order in which money flows back to investors — the distribution waterfall. Does income arrive monthly, quarterly, or on some other schedule? Are distributions discretionary or mandatory when cash is available? And in what order are different parties paid — investors first, or the sponsor alongside you? A clause that puts investor distributions ahead of sponsor performance fees is worth more than any slide about alignment. If the agreement is silent on timing, ask; "as and when determined" can mean very different things in practice.

5. Exit and transfer terms

Private shares are not like listed shares — you cannot sell them with a tap. This clause should explain how you can exit: is there a fixed holding period, a redemption mechanism, a buyback option, or a resale process through the platform? Look for restrictions: lock-up periods, transfer approvals, and any discounts applied to early exits. Understanding this clause before you sign is the difference between a deliberate commitment and an unpleasant surprise years later.

How to read it

Read the agreement end to end at least once, slowly, before you commit. Mark anything you do not understand and ask the platform to explain it in plain language — a good platform will. If a clause is genuinely ambiguous, assume it will be interpreted against you; ambiguity in a contract you did not draft rarely works in your favour.

The subscription agreement is not a formality. It is the only document that binds everyone involved. The five clauses above are where the real deal lives.
