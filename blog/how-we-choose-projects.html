How Projects Get Chosen: Our Evaluation Process.

10 Oct 2026 · By the OwnStakeX team

Most projects never reach the platform. Here is the evaluation process every opportunity goes through — and why saying no is the most important part of it.

When people ask how projects get chosen, they usually expect a story about spotting winners. The honest answer is less glamorous: selection is mostly a filtering process, and most of the work is rejecting deals that do not meet the bar. Here is how that process works, step by step.

Stage one: the basics

Every submission starts with simple questions. Is the asset real and verifiable? Is the legal structure clear — who owns what, and how is ownership transferred? Are the financials presented with enough detail to check, or only in headlines? If the answer to any of these is no, the deal stops here. A surprising number do not survive this first pass.

We also check fit: is this the kind of asset — commercial property, yachts, hospitality — that our investors understand and that we can report on properly? If a project needs expertise we do not have, we do not list it, however attractive the numbers look.

Stage two: the operator

In shared ownership, you back the operator as much as the asset. So we dig into the people running the project: their track record with similar assets, their financial stake in the deal, and what happens to investors if the operator underperforms or walks away. We ask for references and we actually call them.

Alignment matters here. We look for operators who invest their own capital alongside investors, whose fees reward performance rather than just activity, and who are willing to report bad news quickly. An operator who only shows up with good numbers in a pitch is a red flag, not a selling point.

Stage three: the numbers, checked independently

Projected returns are claims until verified. We rebuild the revenue forecast from the operator's assumptions and stress-test them: what if occupancy drops? What if costs run higher? What if the exit takes longer? A project that only works in the best case does not make the cut.

We also review the cost structure line by line — acquisition costs, management fees, maintenance reserves — because fees are where investor returns most often quietly disappear. Everything that will be deducted before distributions must be disclosed and defensible.

Stage four: legal and compliance review

Before any project is listed, the legal structure is reviewed: the subscription agreement, the ownership documents, the register mechanics, and the regulatory position of the offer. KYC and screening standards for investors are set. This stage is slow and unglamorous, and it is non-negotiable.

Why we say no more than we say yes

A platform that lists everything quickly is not doing you a favour — it is shifting the diligence burden onto you. Our job is to carry that burden first. Deals get rejected for inflated projections, unclear ownership, weak operator alignment, fee structures that favour the sponsor, or simply because the numbers do not survive scrutiny.

We do not publish a list of rejected deals — confidentiality binds us there — but the selectivity itself is the point. Every project on the platform survived a process designed to kill it. That is the standard, and we would rather list fewer projects than lower it.
